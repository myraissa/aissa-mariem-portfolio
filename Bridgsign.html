The Problem

A wall of silence — in the middle of a crisis

During the COVID-19 pandemic in Tunisia, a deaf man was detained by police for violating lockdown measures. He had no idea there even was a lockdown — because every public health announcement had been in spoken Arabic or French. Not one was available in Tunisian Sign Language.

Existing "solutions" — animated GIFs, static image dictionaries, human interpreters on broadcast TV — don't scale. They can't handle arbitrary input. They're not real-time. And they don't work for Tunisian Sign Language specifically, which has its own grammar, structure, and non-manual markers entirely distinct from Arabic or French Sign Language.

"The problem wasn't just technical. No dataset existed. No prior system existed. We weren't improving something — we were building from a blank page."

The technical gap was significant: converting spoken or written language into grammatically accurate, biomechanically valid sign language animation requires solving three deeply different problems simultaneously — NLP, computer vision, and real-time 3D rendering — in a pipeline that has to work together seamlessly.

Technical Deep Dive

How it actually works

Every input — typed text, spoken audio, or uploaded video — flows through the same multi-stage pipeline before reaching the avatar.

Core implementation details

NLP & Glossing Built a processing layer using spaCy to parse French and Arabic text into semantic units called "glosses" — the grammatical building blocks of sign language. The layer reorders sentences to match Tunisian Sign Language (LST) grammar, which differs significantly from French or Arabic word order. Augmented with Gemini API for context-aware gloss disambiguation on ambiguous input.
Pose extraction Used MediaPipe Holistic for simultaneous tracking of body pose, face, and both hands (543 landmarks total per frame). Implemented a custom normalization algorithm that makes motion data invariant to camera angle, signer distance, and body proportions — critical for ensuring that a sign recorded by a 1.5m signer plays correctly on any avatar skeleton.
Forward kinematics Raw MediaPipe output gives 3D landmark coordinates, not joint rotations. Unity avatars require Euler angles. I implemented a vector math layer that computes rotation matrices between limb segments relative to a T-pose reference frame, with anatomical joint constraints on finger bending to prevent unnatural "broken" hand poses.
Facial blendshapes Extracted 52 ARKit-compatible blendshapes per frame. Non-manual markers — raised eyebrows for yes/no questions, mouth shapes, head tilt — are grammatically required in sign language, not decorative. Blendshapes are smoothed and streamed to Unity for accurate lip-sync and grammatical facial expression.
Motion smoothing Concatenating discrete pre-recorded sign units creates discontinuities at transition points. Applied Savitzky–Golay filtering to remove high-frequency jitter while preserving sharp motion peaks, combined with cubic spline interpolation to generate smooth transition frames between signs.
API & integration Shipped as a Flask API. The Python pipeline outputs a structured JSON motion schema co-designed with the Unity developer — containing per-frame bone rotations, blend weights, and timing metadata — for real-time WebGL playback at 30 FPS.
Challenges & Solutions

The problems nobody warned me about

Building from scratch means inheriting every unsolved problem too. These were the three that took the most engineering to crack.

Challenge

Coordinates ≠ rotations

MediaPipe gives 3D landmark positions (x, y, z). Unity needs joint rotations (Euler angles). Naively mapping landmarks to transforms results in anatomically impossible poses — limbs that fold backwards, fingers that intersect.

Solution

Vector math rotation layer

Implemented a custom forward kinematics solver: compute the direction vector of each limb segment, calculate its rotation relative to the parent bone in T-pose space, apply anatomical constraints per joint. The avatar now moves like a human hand.

Challenge

The avatar shakes uncontrollably

Frame-by-frame pose inference introduces high-frequency noise. When signs are concatenated, the avatar vibrates at transitions. Early demos looked like the avatar was having a seizure — unusable and incomprehensible to the deaf audience.

Solution

Adaptive filtering + interpolation

Applied Savitzky–Golay filtering (preserves peak motion during fast signs, smooths noise during slow holds) and cubic spline interpolation at transition boundaries. Jitter became invisible. Motion felt natural.

Challenge

LST grammar ≠ French grammar

Direct word-for-word text-to-sign translation produces nonsense. Tunisian Sign Language has its own topic-comment structure, spatial grammar, and drops grammatical words. A naive pipeline produces signs in the wrong order, incomprehensible to a native signer.

Solution

Grammar transformation layer

Built a dedicated NLP stage that parses input text, extracts semantic content, reorders it according to LST grammar rules, and maps to glosses. Used spaCy for parsing and Gemini API for disambiguation in ambiguous or idiomatic cases.

What I Learned

What this project taught me that no course does

  • 1

    Domain knowledge beats model accuracy. A 95% accurate pose estimator that produces anatomically impossible hand poses is worthless. Understanding biomechanics well enough to write joint constraints mattered more than any model metric.

  • 2

    Linguistic validity is not optional. AI engineers building translation systems who don't deeply understand the target language produce systems that are technically impressive and practically useless. I had to learn sign language grammar — not just build a model.

  • 3

    Real-time constraints change everything. A pipeline that works at 2 FPS in a notebook is not a pipeline. Every processing stage had to be profiled, and several elegant solutions had to be replaced with faster, uglier ones. That's engineering, not research.

  • 4

    Cross-disciplinary collaboration is a technical skill. Coordinating with a Unity developer on a JSON motion schema, and with linguistic experts on gloss grammar rules, required me to translate between technical languages — not just write code.
